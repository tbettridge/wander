    Fable · Full technical design

    Agentive NPC commitments, consequences, and social memory

    • Draft for review
    • Owner Tim Bettridge
    • Author Codex with Tim Bettridge
    • Reviewers Gameplay, narrative, simulation

    TL;DR

    Give each travelling NPC a concrete, deterministic commitment and make its completion change authoritative world state. Add directed relationships and entity-addressed social memories so NPCs can remember one another and pass bounded, sourced rumors during real NPC conversations. A local world ledger owns facts and applies every outcome exactly once; the on-device language model expresses those facts but never creates or mutates them. Ship this in seven gated phases, beginning with state and observability, then one delivery vertical slice, the remaining outcome types, and finally social propagation.

    Context & problem

    Fable's NPCs can travel, talk, remember the player, notice passers-by, and visually converse with one another. The simulation still stops one step short of agency: an NPC's intention does not survive as a world fact, arrival changes nothing, and one person's knowledge cannot become another person's knowledge.

    Current behavior

    • npcjourney.mjs chooses a destination and purpose, moves a traveller, then clears the route on arrival. Its purpose is a narrative seed rather than an actionable commitment.
    • stationkeeper.js owns journeys, player conversations, and visual NPC-to-NPC conversations. Those social encounters animate gestures and turn-taking but exchange no semantic information.
    • npcmemory.mjs stores bounded strings per NPC about the player, the speaker, quests, landmarks, and world facts. Entries cannot refer reliably to another NPC or preserve who supplied a claim.
    • livingworld.mjs correctly prevents the model from claiming it changed the world. Consequently, even a compelling story about a letter, trade, reunion, or repair cannot become true.
    • Stable NPC IDs already exist as npc:<station-id>:<slot-key>, and journeys already advance outside render range. These are strong foundations for persistent intent.

    The missing causal chain

    An alive-seeming person needs a legible chain: want → commitment → action → consequence → recollection → retelling. Today Fable renders the action but drops the commitment before it can produce a consequence. The player therefore cannot verify that a stated intention mattered, encounter its result later, or hear news travel through the population.

    Target player experience

    The system succeeds when players can infer causality without opening a debug panel. The first release must support these four grounded vignettes:

    CommitmentBefore and duringConsequence on arrivalLater evidence
    Courier deliveryThe courier carries a specific letter for a named recipient at a known destination and may state its deadline.Letter ownership moves exactly once to the recipient; both participants remember the delivery.The recipient can mention the letter and the sender; another NPC may later repeat that it arrived.
    Trader restockThe trader carries a typed bundle of goods to a station.The station's small, abstract inventory projection changes by a deterministic quantity.Station dialogue and debug readouts reflect the new stock; a later trade may consume it.
    Personal visitThe visitor travels to meet a concrete NPC, not an invented relative.When both are co-located, each gains familiarity and a memory of the meeting.Either person can refer to the visit, and the relationship affects future greetings and rumor sharing.
    Worker repairThe worker targets a concrete damaged asset or maintenance site.Arrival creates an in-progress repair job; visible work begins and completes after a simulated duration.The repaired projection persists, and witnesses can truthfully report who repaired it.

    Acceptance ruleEvery consequence must be observable through authoritative state and at least one in-world channel—animation/prop, changed dialogue, changed station or asset state, or a later sourced memory. Debug UI alone is insufficient.

    Goals & non-goals

    Goals

    • Represent every intentional journey with an actor, target, destination, deadline, lifecycle state, and terminal outcome.
    • Apply delivery, trade, visit, and repair consequences exactly once, whether arrival is visible, culled, or followed by a reload.
    • Store directed NPC relationships and memories that reference stable entities and preserve provenance.
    • Spread a small number of relevant rumors during actual NPC social encounters without unbounded duplication.
    • Expose the same facts to authored dialogue and on-device dialogue while keeping simulation authority deterministic.
    • Remain bounded, seed-repeatable, testable without Three.js, and inexpensive for off-screen actors.

    Non-goals

    • A general-purpose autonomous-agent framework, unrestricted planning, or model tool-calling.
    • A full economy, crafting system, global item simulation, or physically modelled supply chain.
    • Generated quest chains for the player, combat, crime, faction politics, romance, or reproduction.
    • Automatic sentiment analysis of free-form dialogue or relationship changes based on model interpretation.
    • Advancing the simulation while the game is closed; v1 pauses fictional time between sessions.
    • Deliberate rumor mutation, lying, secrets gameplay, or fact-checking arbitrary natural-language claims in v1.

    Design principles & decisions

    DecisionThe simulation owns truth; language owns expression. Commitment creation, targets, effects, relationships, and claim provenance are deterministic data. A model may phrase or interpret a bounded conversation, but its output never directly mutates the ledger.

    1. Concrete references over prose. NPCs, places, assets, items, and commitments use stable IDs; display names are presentation.
    2. One primary commitment per NPC in v1. This keeps behavior legible and state transitions finite. Ambient desires may be added later.
    3. Movement executes intent; it does not invent intent. The commitment planner selects a valid destination and asks the journey layer to execute the route.
    4. Outcomes are transactions. A stable event ID is validated and reduced atomically into the same persisted snapshot as its receipt.
    5. Knowledge is subjective. Relationships are directed, and every social memory belongs to one NPC with a source and confidence.
    6. Uncertainty is retained, not flattened. Dialogue distinguishes observed events, things heard from another person, player claims, and legacy narrative memory.
    7. Bound every growing collection. Memories, event history, commitment history, and rumor hops have explicit caps and deterministic eviction.
    8. No renderer dependency. Planning, state transitions, outcomes, memory exchange, persistence, and soak tests run headlessly.

    Proposed architecture

    Add a small authoritative state layer between NPC intention and the existing journey renderer. Preserve the current renderer, gait, grounding, and dialogue director as consumers.

    Runtime flow

    1. The planner selects a commitment template allowed by the NPC's role, resolves only existing entity IDs, calculates a route, and records a deadline with slack.
    2. The journey executor moves the NPC exactly as it does today but carries commitmentId and emits structured transitions before clearing arrival data.
    3. The outcome resolver validates that the actor, expected destination, target, payload, and commitment state still match.
    4. The world ledger applies one deterministic event transaction, records its receipt, updates projections, and resolves or blocks the commitment.
    5. The same event seeds relationship changes and first-person memories for participants and eligible witnesses.
    6. NPC social encounters may transfer selected memories as rumors. Player dialogue receives a read-only digest of commitment, relationship, and sourced memory data.

    Commitments and lifecycle

    Commitment record

    {
  version: 1,
  id: "commitment:<world-seed>:<actor-id>:<sequence>",
  actorId: "npc:station-wren:porter",
  kind: "delivery" | "trade" | "visit" | "repair",
  target: { kind: "npc" | "station" | "asset", id: "..." },
  destination: { kind: "landmark" | "station", key: "..." },
  createdAtHour: 142.25,
  deadlineHour: 149.0,               // nullable only for migrated journeys
  state: "planned" | "active" | "blocked" | "resolved",
  priority: 0.0,                     // normalized 0..1
  purposeKey: "deliver-personal-letter",
  payload: { kind: "letter", id: "letter:...", senderId: "npc:..." },
  journeyId: "journey:...",         // set on activation
  blocked: null | { code: "target-absent", sinceHour: 146.1, attempts: 1 },
  outcome: null | {
    status: "succeeded" | "failed" | "cancelled",
    code: "delivered",
    atHour: 146.4,
    placeKey: "landmark:...",
    effectEventIds: ["event:<commitment-id>:delivered"]
  }
}

    purposeKey selects authored presentation and outcome rules. It is not free-form model prose. payload is a small tagged union validated per commitment kind; v1 does not introduce a general item system.

    Planning policy

    • Start with one deterministic template table keyed by role capabilities: couriers/porters deliver, vendors trade, residents visit, and keepers/workers repair.
    • Seed selection from (worldSeed, actorId, commitmentSequence). Candidate order must be stable before random selection.
    • Resolve named targets from the complete NPC registry, not only spawned avatars. Resolve destinations from the navigation graph.
    • Compute deadline from expected route time, action duration, and template slack. A missed deadline changes the terminal outcome; it never deletes the journey retroactively.
    • Keep the current random journey behavior as a flagged fallback during migration. When commitments are enabled, the journey layer receives a chosen destination rather than calling its own destination picker.

    Consequential arrivals and exactly-once effects

    Journey transition contract

    Today advanceJourney() returns only a phase and clears destination data during arrival. Add a bounded transition queue to journey state and a drainJourneyTransitions(journey) API. The arrival transition captures journeyId, commitmentId, origin, destination, arrival hour, purpose, and position before mutation. Existing callers that only need the phase remain compatible.

    advanceJourney(journey, tick);
for (const transition of drainJourneyTransitions(journey)) {
  commitmentSystem.acceptJourneyTransition(actor.id, transition);
}

    Transactional reducer

    Every effect uses a stable ID derived from the commitment and effect kind, for example event:<commitment-id>:delivered. applyEventOnce() validates first, clones only affected projections, applies reducers, records the event receipt, increments the state revision, and persists one snapshot. If the receipt already exists, it returns the prior result without applying deltas again.

    KindPreconditionsAuthoritative effectsVisible action
    DeliveryActor owns payload; destination matches; recipient exists and is present or reachable by policy.Transfer letter ownership; resolve commitment; seed sender/courier/recipient memories; adjust recipient familiarity with courier.Brief handoff when rendered; recipient later acknowledges letter.
    TradeActor owns typed goods; target is destination station.Consume cargo; increment bounded station inventory projection; record restock event.Unload gesture/prop; vendor or keeper comments on changed stock.
    VisitActor and target NPC are co-located within a social radius.Record meeting; increment directed familiarity on both edges; seed distinct first-person memories.Start a semantic social encounter rather than immediately resolving at the map coordinate.
    RepairTarget asset exists and remains damaged or due for maintenance.Create a repair job and hold the commitment active during work; completion changes asset projection and resolves commitment.Worker moves to work anchor and loops a repair gesture for an authored duration.

    Absent targets, deadlines, and retries

    • An absent NPC target blocks the commitment. The default policy waits for a short authored window, then replans once to the target's authoritative current destination, and finally fails at its deadline.
    • A trade target and repair asset are stationary. If missing after world migration, resolve as failed:target-removed and retain a tombstone outcome for dialogue.
    • Arrival after the deadline may still apply a physical delivery but records succeeded-late; templates decide whether lateness changes relationship deltas.
    • Off-screen effects apply immediately. If the player arrives later, a short “aftermath” presentation may surface the latest consequential event; it does not replay the transaction.

    Relationship model

    Relationships are directed because one person's trust or affection need not be reciprocated. Store a sparse edge only after a meaningful interaction; missing edges read as neutral strangers.

    {
  version: 1,
  ownerId: "npc:station-wren:porter",
  subjectId: "npc:station-ash:keeper",
  familiarity: 0.0,     // 0..1; grows through contact, decays slowly if desired later
  affinity: 0.0,        // -1..1; event-driven, not model sentiment
  trust: 0.0,           // -1..1; influences whether reports are believed
  obligation: 0.0,      // -1..1; positive means owner feels they owe subject
  tags: ["family"],     // authored/validated tags only
  lastInteractionHour: 146.4,
  lastEventId: "event:..."
}

    Relationship reducers

    • Co-location and first meetings raise familiarity modestly; repetition has diminishing returns.
    • Successful deliveries, visits, help, and fulfilled commitments apply small template-defined deltas. Late or failed commitments can reduce trust.
    • Family, employment, and existing acquaintance edges may be seeded deterministically from the world roster, but they do not imply symmetric feelings.
    • Player relationships use the stable pseudo-entity player:local and change only from validated gameplay events in v1—not from whether the model judged a sentence polite.
    • All numeric fields clamp in reducers; no prompt or UI writes an edge directly.

    Dialogue receives a compact qualitative band—stranger, familiar, trusted, wary, indebted—plus a few relevant event references. Raw scores are never spoken or exposed to the model.

    Entity-addressed social memory

    Replace free-floating rumor strings with bounded claims that identify who remembers what, who or what the claim concerns, and how the owner learned it. Keep existing narrative strings through migration as explicitly unstructured legacy memories.

    {
  version: 2,
  id: "memory:<owner-id>:<lineage-id>",
  ownerId: "npc:station-ash:keeper",
  subject: { kind: "npc", id: "npc:station-wren:porter" },
  predicate: "commitment.outcome",
  object: { commitmentId: "commitment:...", status: "succeeded", code: "delivered" },
  summaryKey: "heard-delivery-succeeded",
  source: { kind: "npc", id: "npc:station-wren:porter" },
  provenance: "observed" | "told" | "player-claim" | "legacy",
  originEventId: "event:...",       // null only for player-claim or legacy
  lineageId: "claim:event:...",
  confidence: 0.86,                 // 0..1
  salience: 0.72,                   // 0..1
  privacy: "public" | "personal" | "private",
  hopCount: 1,
  createdAtHour: 146.4,
  lastRecalledHour: 149.1,
  expiresAtHour: 221.0              // nullable for durable personal events
}

    Creation and validation

    • World events create observed memories for direct participants and nearby witnesses. The event itself is the authoritative source.
    • A transferred rumor creates a new subjective memory for the listener with the speaker as source and the same lineageId.
    • Player statements extracted from a transcript are stored as player-claim, never promoted to world fact solely because the model repeated them.
    • The language model may propose a compact summary for presentation, but IDs, predicates, provenance, and confidence are created or validated by deterministic code.
    • Dedupe on (ownerId, lineageId). Hearing the same claim again refreshes recency and may adjust confidence; it does not add another entry.

    Capacity and forgetting

    Cap v2 social memories at 32 per NPC. Protect active commitments, unresolved obligations, and the latest direct interaction with the player. Evict expired entries first, then the lowest deterministic score over salience, confidence, recency, and personal relevance. A tie breaks by memory ID so save/reload order cannot change the result.

    Rumor propagation during NPC conversations

    Use the existing semantic moment—an NPC-to-NPC conversation created by stationkeeper.js and advanced by npcsocial.mjs—rather than inventing a separate invisible gossip timer.

    Exchange algorithm

    1. At the first delivered conversational beat, create one social.exchange event for the pair. Repeated animation updates cannot create another exchange for that conversation ID.
    2. Build each speaker's candidate set from memories the listener does not already own, excluding private entries and claims below confidence 0.35.
    3. Score relevance from salience, recency, relationship to listener, shared place/people, and whether the subject is currently nearby. Use stable seeded jitter only to break monotony.
    4. Transfer zero to two claims per participant. “Zero” remains valid so every visual chat does not become an information firehose.
    5. Set listener confidence to sourceConfidence × (0.72 + 0.18 × normalizedTrust), increment hopCount, and preserve lineage and origin.
    6. Reject transfers beyond three hops in v1, dedupe cycles, then emit a brief gesture or authored subtitle only if the exchange is visible.

    What is and is not allowed to spread

    Allowed in v1Held back
    Commitment departures and outcomes, arrivals, meetings, repairs, stock changes, player claims, and public place observations.Private memories, raw prompt text, arbitrary model inventions, system instructions, exact coordinates, and expired claims.
    Uncertain phrasing that preserves provenance: “Wren told me…”, “I heard…”, “the traveller claimed…”.Deliberate lying, mutation of structured claims, or promotion from hearsay to authoritative state.

    DecisionRumor spread copies knowledge, not truth. A widely repeated claim can remain false or unverified; repetition changes familiarity and confidence only within bounded rules.

    World state, time, and persistence

    One versioned world snapshot

    Persist authoritative living-world state under wander.livingWorld.state.v1.<worldSeed>. The snapshot contains schema version, world seed, state revision, simulation clock, NPC registry version, open and recent commitments, outcome projections, relationship edges, social memories, and effect receipts. Keeping transaction state and receipts in one localStorage.setItem prevents partial multi-key commits.

    • Batch ordinary writes to at most once per second. Flush terminal outcomes and page-hide state immediately where supported.
    • Keep only 12 resolved commitments per NPC, but retain their terminal effect IDs inside the same record. Pruning removes the record and its receipt together after no queued event can refer to it.
    • Save failure degrades to session-only behavior and raises one debug warning; it never resets valid in-memory state.
    • Parse and migrate through explicit schema functions. Preserve corrupt raw data for diagnostics in memory, fall back safely, and never silently overwrite it during the failing load.

    Clock semantics

    Add a shared LivingWorldClock that exposes monotonic activeSeconds and worldHours. Journeys continue using active seconds for locomotion and world hours for loitering; deadlines use world hours. The planner estimates deadline slack using route walking time and the current world-time rate. Both clocks pause while the simulation is inactive, and v1 performs no wall-clock catch-up between sessions.

    Identity and world migration

    Stable NPC IDs already derive from world seed, station ID, and authored slot key. Add an entity registry snapshot so missing stations, slots, or assets become tombstones rather than dangling references. A world-generation compatibility version decides whether a prior snapshot migrates or starts a clearly labelled new living-world state.

    Dialogue and model boundary

    Read-only context

    Extend LivingWorldPopulation.context() with a compact social block:

    social: {
  activeCommitment: describeCommitment(commitment, registry),
  recentOutcomes: describeOutcomes(actorId, 3),
  relationshipToPlayer: "familiar",
  relevantPeople: [{ id, name, role, relationship: "trusted" }],
  memories: [{ statement, provenance, sourceName, confidenceBand }]
}
    • The context builder resolves IDs to current names and uses authored phrase templates. The model receives no internal scores or mutable APIs.
    • Update the system prompt so commitment specifics—recipient, payload, destination, status, deadline—are anchors, not details the model may invent. Texture and feelings remain creative space.
    • Require sourced language for rumors and player claims. Direct observations may be stated confidently; legacy memories are labelled fallible.
    • Authored fallback dialogue consumes the identical context and must support all four commitment/outcome types without on-device AI.
    • Memory synthesis returns proposals only. Deterministic validation verifies referenced entity aliases occurred in context/transcript and marks player assertions unverified.

    Prompt safetySocial memories are data, not instructions. Serialize them in a clearly delimited “fallible memory” block, normalize text length, and retain the existing instruction that attempts to change role or reveal prompts are merely odd statements made by the traveller.

    Dependency rule

    Pure state modules may depend on noise/hash utilities and data-only trail helpers. They must not import Three.js, DOM code, stationkeeper.js, or the language-model director. Presentation imports state—not the reverse.

    Testing, observability, and quality gates

    Automated verification

    • State-machine tests: enumerate allowed/forbidden commitment transitions and require a terminal outcome exactly once.
    • Property tests: random duplicate, reordered, delayed, and replayed events must converge to the same projection and never double inventory or relationship deltas.
    • Scenario integration tests: run delivery, trade, visit, and repair from planning through save/reload and dialogue context, both visible and culled.
    • Rumor invariants: no duplicate owner/lineage pair, no private transfer, no hop above three, deterministic selection, stable eviction, and bounded memory under cycles.
    • Migration tests: import existing v1 per-NPC memory and an in-flight legacy journey without losing established text facts.
    • Soak test: simulate 64 NPCs for 1,000 world hours, including target movement and save/reload checkpoints; require finite state, no dangling IDs, and bounded snapshot size.
    • Fallback parity: run all grounded dialogue/context tests with the language model unavailable.
    • Regression: make npm test execute the complete supported test set so new suites cannot be omitted from the default check.

    Debug surfaces

    • Selected NPC panel: active commitment, target, deadline, state, route, latest outcome, relationship bands, and top sourced memories.
    • World timeline: recent events with stable IDs, applied/duplicate disposition, place, participants, and projection deltas.
    • Rumor inspector: lineage, source chain, hop count, confidence band, recipients, and rejection reason.
    • Aggregate counters: open/blocked/resolved commitments, effect dedupes, memory evictions, exchanges, snapshot bytes, save failures, and simulation milliseconds.

    Debug logging is opt-in. Normal gameplay should not emit per-frame or per-NPC console noise.

    Risks & open questions

    RiskState-space growth. Four commitment types multiplied by missing targets, lateness, save/reload, and visibility can become combinatorial. Mitigate with one shared lifecycle, typed preconditions, one delivery vertical slice first, property tests, and no secondary commitments in v1.

    RiskConsequences happen unseen and feel imaginary. Apply effects off-screen for correctness, then retain a bounded aftermath cue and surface outcomes through props, participant dialogue, and rumors when the player next enters the area.

    RiskRumor echo chambers and repetitive dialogue. Use lineage dedupe, hop and capacity caps, zero-to-two transfer, relevance scoring, cooldowns, source-aware phrasing, and debug rejection reasons.

    RiskIdentity drift after world-generation changes. Version the entity registry, preserve tombstones, test stable station/slot IDs, and require an explicit compatibility migration rather than silently binding an old relationship to a new person.

    RiskPrompted claims escape into world truth. Keep model output read-only, validate extracted references, mark player statements as claims, and derive every authoritative memory from a ledger event.

    RiskPersistence quota or corruption. Cap all collections, track snapshot bytes, batch writes, keep session behavior alive on failure, and add explicit migration/corruption fixtures before default-on.

    Feature flags and rollback

    Use nested flags: commitmentsEnabled, consequencesEnabled, socialMemoryEnabled, and rumorExchangeEnabled. Disabling a consumer stops new behavior without deleting its snapshot. The old journey-purpose and v1 memory readers remain available through Phase 6; remove them only after a migration release has proven stable.
