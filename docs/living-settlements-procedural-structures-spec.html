    Living settlements

    • In review
    • Author Codex with Tim Bettridge
    • Reviewers World generation, gameplay, NPC simulation, rendering, performance
    • Updated 2026-08-02

    TL;DR

    Generate farmsteads, hamlets, villages, and towns as purpose-driven world sites whose buildings, paths, households, workplaces, and daily movement agree with one authoritative plan. Buildings are program-first and parametrically varied, every visible door is a real portal, interiors occupy the same world coordinates as exteriors, and NPCs use one layered route from regional trail to room. Ship in seven gated phases: establish deterministic site contracts, prove one complete farmstead, generalize structures and portals, add households, add consequential work routines, scale to larger settlements, then enable sparse evolution and default-on streaming.

    Context & problem

    Fable's NPCs can now travel with concrete intentions and produce real outcomes, but the places around them do not yet support a lived life.

    The current world is strong at wilderness-scale meaning: deterministic landmarks, paths connecting stable landmark keys, walkable crossings, a regional railway, off-screen NPC journeys, commitments, relationships, rumor memory, situated actions, and bounded persistence. It does not yet have a shared representation for a home, a workplace, an address, a doorway, a room, a household, or the local paths that connect them.

    • Procedural structures can become scenery instead of places. A random shell may look varied while having no sensible rooms, no reachable door, no work function, and no reason to exist there.
    • Interiors can split the world in two. Pocket dimensions or teleport interiors simplify rendering but make NPC routes, arrivals, visibility, and player pursuit harder to reason about.
    • Families can become labels. Assigning “wife” or “brother” without shared space, routines, memories, obligations, and time together will not make a household feel real.
    • Commuting can become decorative. Walking to work matters only if arrival staffs a shop, starts a repair, changes inventory, opens a service, or creates a remembered absence.
    • Larger settlements can break the contemplative world. Uniform building scatter, excessive population, fake doors, and frame hitches would make towns feel less believable than a single carefully structured farmstead.

    The design problem is therefore not “generate many buildings.” It is to generate a coherent social place whose terrain, silhouette, paths, doors, rooms, inhabitants, schedules, consequences, rendering, and saved history all derive from compatible data.

    Goals & non-goals

    Goals

    • Generate recognizably different farmsteads, hamlets, villages, and towns from terrain, purpose, prosperity, history, and inhabitants.
    • Give every resident a stable home location and household; support partners, spouses, relatives, siblings, multigenerational homes, lodgers, and single-person homes.
    • Make every visible building door functional for the player and NPCs, with a reachable interior that fits its exterior envelope.
    • Route player and NPCs continuously from regional trail through settlement path, door, and room using shared portal and collision authority.
    • Give working NPCs a stable workplace, schedule, commute, and observable arrival consequence.
    • Make settlements major navigational landmarks with a founding reason, approach silhouette, trail entrance, and local circulation.
    • Keep generation deterministic, streaming bounded, saves compact, feature rollback independent, and model-generated language non-authoritative.

    Non-goals

    • Player-authored construction, voxel destruction, structural physics, fire propagation, or fully destructible settlements.
    • A complete monetary economy, supply-chain simulation, agriculture calendar, genetics, childbirth, or lifetime demographic simulation in v1.
    • Thousands of individually simulated citizens; large populations use deterministic summaries and simulation tiers.
    • Arbitrary skyscrapers or unrestricted multi-storey navigation; v1 structures are one to two storeys with validated stairs.
    • Photorealistic architecture, bespoke geometry for every seed, or runtime AI-generated meshes.
    • Fake façade doors, inaccessible “hero” homes, or exterior shells whose interior dimensions cannot exist.
    • Replacing existing wilderness landmarks, cave routes, station systems, or commitment journeys.

    Experience and design principles

    1. Generate from purpose and people. First decide why the settlement exists and who lives or works there; lots, programs, buildings, paths, props, and routines follow.
    2. A door is a promise. If a mesh reads as a door, it opens or communicates a grounded reason it cannot. Decorative fake doors are prohibited.
    3. One physical world. Interior floors and rooms use world coordinates inside the exterior envelope. Crossing a threshold changes visibility and acoustic context, not location identity.
    4. Landmark outside, life inside. A settlement must read from far away through silhouette and approach cues, then reward proximity with yards, thresholds, rooms, people, and evidence of routine.
    5. Schedules create meetings, not rails. Home and work are recurring obligations with windows and recovery behavior. Commitments, conversations, groups, weather, and safety may interrupt them.
    6. Consequences prove agency. A worker's arrival changes a staffed station, task, service, repair, or inventory projection. If nothing changes, the commute is ambient animation and should not be simulated as work.
    7. Deterministic base, sparse history. Regenerate the unchanged world from seed and generator version; save only meaningful differences and exactly-once outcomes.
    8. Density without noise. Larger settlements add hierarchy and recognizable districts before they add more independently animated people.

    Every generated building has at least one real portal and reachable room graph. Small prop-scale objects that are intentionally non-enterable—hayricks, animal pens, lean-to covers—must not use a door silhouette or building classification.

    Purpose-driven placement and landmark integration

    World-site contract

    Introduce a THREE-free WorldSiteSummary interface consumed by landmarks, trails, vegetation, navigation, dialogue, and settlement streaming. Existing landmark and cave keys remain valid; settlement keys use a distinct stable namespace such as settlement:3_-2.

    WorldSiteSummary {
  id, key, kind, seed, generationVersion,
  x, y, z, yaw, bounds, exclusionHalo,
  purpose, settlementClass, identity,
  regionalEntrance: { id, x, z, heading },
  trailWeight, silhouetteCue, planHash
}

    The trail solver is generalized from “landmark endpoint” to TrailAnchor while retaining fromKey, toKey, halo endpoints, and canonical edge IDs. A primary regional trail terminates at the settlement entrance, never at its visual center. Local roads and lanes begin at that exact handoff.

    DecisionSettlement placement must be pure and shared between main thread and workers, like landmarks today. Meshes, residents, and mutable state are consumers of the plan; none may choose their own footprint.

    Program-first parametric structures

    Variation is generated around a valid use, not by perturbing a decorative box until it looks different.

    Generation pipeline

    1. Site constraints: terrain samples, water, trail/rail approaches, sunlight, prevailing wind, protected landmark/cave halos, and buildable slope.
    2. Settlement diagram: entrance, circulation spine, common space, districts, lots, field/work zones, landmark view corridors, and drainage.
    3. Lot program: household/workplace demand, public/private frontage, yard needs, deliveries, animal or tool access, and prosperity.
    4. Room graph: required rooms, adjacency, minimum dimensions, privacy, occupancy, work anchors, stairs, and portal connections.
    5. Footprint and massing: solve the room graph inside one or more compatible rectangles/wings, adapt foundation to grade, then derive roof form and storeys.
    6. Openings: place authoritative exterior doors first, interior doors second, then windows and chimneys around structure and furniture clearance.
    7. Envelope and material language: regional material family, age, prosperity, repairs, weather exposure, roof pitch, eaves, trim, and controlled asymmetry.
    8. Furniture and action anchors: beds, hearth, table, storage, counter, workbench, repair site, shelter, conversation positions, and item surfaces.
    9. Validation: collision-free lots, every required room reachable, door swing/threshold clear, stairs valid, roof contains rooms, no water/cliff intrusion, and entrance connected to the local path graph.

    Stable variation channels

    Use independently hashed channels—site, lots, program, rooms, envelope, materials, details, weathering, and furnishings—rather than one consumed RNG stream. Changing chimney options must not reroll a household's address or move the front door.

    Doors, interiors, collision, and visibility

    Real-space interiors

    Interior geometry is built inside the exterior footprint at the same world position and scale. The player and NPC never teleport on entry. Near a building, interior surfaces stream with the exterior; crossing its threshold changes render groups, acoustics, weather exposure, and interaction context.

    • Outside: exterior shell is authoritative; interior detail may be unloaded or depth/portal culled.
    • Threshold: both sides remain available; door, porch, floor claims, and audio transition agree.
    • Inside: roof and selected enclosing faces use an interior visibility policy appropriate to camera/XR mode; nearby exterior context remains visible through the doorway.
    • Windows may be visually simplified in v1, but cannot imply a traversable opening unless registered as a portal.

    Authoritative portal state

    PortalRecord {
  id, buildingId, kind: "door" | "interior-door" | "stair",
  fromLocationId, toLocationId,
  position, heading, width, clearance,
  access: "public" | "threshold" | "guest" | "household" | "workforce",
  state: "open" | "closed" | "locked" | "blocked",
  reservation: { actorId, expiresAtHour } | null,
  version, lastEventId
}

    The discrete portal state owns collision and route availability. Animated openAmount is presentation-only and converges on that state. Player and NPC calls use the same reducer. NPCs reserve a narrow threshold before approach, open it if permitted, cross, then release it; stale reservations expire deterministically.

    Ground and collision authority

    Buildings publish foundation, porch, floor, stair, and threshold claims to WalkableSurface and collision volumes to one settlement collision index. Register/unregister is tied to stream lifetime. A door collider changes from the portal state; the visual mesh never independently decides whether passage is open.

    Honesty ruleA locked or private door remains functional: it responds, explains its access state, and may be opened by an authorized resident. “Locked” is world data, not a disguised missing interior.

    Homes, households, families, and privacy

    Every canonical resident has a home, but not every resident must be married or part of a nuclear family.

    Household model

    HouseholdRecord {
  id, settlementId, homeBuildingId,
  memberIds, relationshipEdgeIds,
  roomAssignments: { actorId: { bedAnchorId, storageAnchorId? } },
  sharedAssets, hospitalityPolicy,
  mealWindows, householdState, generationVersion
}

    Household composition is generated from dwelling capacity and settlement context: partnered adults with or without other relatives, siblings sharing a holding, multigenerational adults, a resident with a lodger, unrelated workers sharing rooms, or a single resident. Existing directed affinity/trust remains; add explicit symmetric or paired kinship/partnership tags and stable household membership rather than encoding family in surnames alone.

    Domestic life

    • Residents know their threshold, bed/storage assignment, common room, household members, and expected presence windows.
    • Meals, arriving home, leaving for work, waiting up, shared chores, and conversations are typed household episodes selected through the existing activity arbiter.
    • Co-residents can exchange sourced memories and rumors when they actually overlap at home; relationship effects are event-driven and bounded.
    • A missing or late member creates a grounded observation only when another member was present to notice.
    • Household changes—new lodger, separation, inheritance, damage displacement—are sparse evolution events, not daily regeneration.

    Privacy and hospitality

    Workplaces, recurring obligations, and consequential commuting

    Home/work rhythms repeat; commitments resolve once. Model them separately so a morning shift does not look like an endless chain of disposable commitments.

    RoutineObligation {
  id, actorId, kind: "work" | "home" | "meal" | "service",
  origin, destination, scheduleWindow,
  priority, requiredRole?, taskAnchorId?,
  state: "scheduled" | "preparing" | "travelling" | "present" | "missed" | "recovering",
  occurrenceKey, interruption, lastOutcome
}

WorkplaceRecord {
  id, buildingId, kind, roleSlots,
  shiftWindows, taskAnchors, inventoryId?,
  serviceState, staffing, productionRules
}

    Priority and interruption

    Add routine at priority 35: above ambient/situated activity, below an active commitment, offer, group, dialogue, or safety claim. Urgency rises only within a bounded departure window; it never overrides safety or forcibly ends player dialogue. After interruption, a routine re-plans from the actor's real location and may become late or missed.

    Arrival outcomes

    Work kindArrival consequenceAbsence / late consequence
    Shop, inn, stallService opens or gains capacity; inventory/trade interaction becomes available.Service remains closed or limited; waiting visitor may remember or report the absence.
    Repair/craftWorker claims bench/site; repair or production progress begins and may consume materials.Repair deadline slips; damaged asset stays visibly unresolved.
    Farm/barnField, animal, storage, or delivery task changes a small projection or prop state.Task is deferred; no invented catastrophe from one missed shift.
    Rail/goodsPlatform or goods role is staffed; parcel/inventory handoffs may resolve.Reduced service capacity and a grounded station observation.
    Public serviceHall, market, or meeting episode gains its required participant.Episode delays, proceeds at reduced capacity, or cancels with an event receipt.

    Consequences use occurrence keys such as routine:npc-id:work:day-42:morning so off-screen catch-up, reload, or duplicate arrival cannot apply the same shift twice. Dialogue describes only the authoritative schedule, route, presence, and outcome.

    Feature flags and rollback

    Add independent flags for settlementsEnabled, enterableBuildingsEnabled, householdsEnabled, workRoutinesEnabled, largeSettlementsEnabled, and settlementEvolutionEnabled. Disabling a consumer preserves compatible state and falls back to a safe exterior/ambient representation; it never deletes household, item, relationship, or portal history.

    Data, persistence, migration, and evolution

    Generated base versus mutable delta

    Regenerate from seedPersist as sparse state
    Site position/class/purpose, lots, unchanged buildings, room graphs, canonical portals, base households, base workplaces, local path graph, silhouettes.Changed ownership/membership, relationship history, portal access/state, damage/repair, construction stage, service/inventory state, displacement, active routines, meaningful outcomes, effect receipts.

    LivingWorldState advances to v4 with collections for settlement deltas, portals, households, workplaces, routines, occupancy, and evolution receipts. Records include generationVersion and planHash. The runtime rebuilds the base plan, validates the hash, overlays deltas, and quarantines unresolved references rather than silently attaching them to a different room.

    Generator compatibility

    • Stable semantic IDs derive from site/lot/building/room role, not array index alone.
    • Patch releases may add presentation detail without changing the plan hash.
    • Layout-changing generator versions require an explicit migration that maps old semantic locations to new ones or pins the old plan version for existing saves.
    • If a delta target disappears, preserve it in an orphan audit and choose a safe fallback location; never lose an NPC, item, or household silently.

    Settlement evolution

    Evolution is event-driven and intentionally slow. Supported v1 changes are repair/damage state, service opening, one construction/upgrade slot, ownership or household reassignment, and displacement after a grounded event. A settlement may progress from farmstead to hamlet only through a versioned expansion plan that preserves existing building IDs and trail entrance; arbitrary daily rerolls are prohibited.

    BudgetThe legacy 64-NPC soak must remain at or below 256 KiB. A farmstead-enabled fixture targets ≤300 KiB total; the worst supported active-town/64-detailed-NPC/1,000-hour fixture targets ≤512 KiB, with deterministic base plans excluded from serialization.

    Streaming, rendering, and simulation tiers

    Three representations, one plan

    TierPresentationSimulation
    Regional summaryTerrain imprint, roof/silhouette clusters, smoke/landmark cue using no new far dynamic lights.Population counts, service summary, next household/work transitions.
    Settlement exteriorStreamed lots, merged shells, paths, yards, props, portals, near residents.Local routes, routine arrivals, groups, household overlap, active services.
    Active buildingInterior surfaces/furnishings, exact portal animation/collision, room acoustics and detail.Room positions, portal reservations, situated actions, conversations, transfers.
    • Pure settlement/building plans may be generated in workers. THREE meshes and GPU-facing resources assemble on the main thread through a strict per-frame queue, like staggered NPC spawning.
    • Reuse merged geometry and instanced detail families by material/LOD. A deterministic priority queue builds the player-facing approach and active portal before rear lots.
    • All canonical residents exist as deterministic summaries, but detailed avatars are capped and selected by proximity, route relevance, commitments, household episode, and service role.
    • Interior unload cannot evict the building containing the player, an active conversation, or an in-view threshold crossing.
    • XR may reduce detail instances and active resident count, but cannot remove collision, doors, required rooms, or authoritative outcomes.

    Testing, observability, and exit-gate strategy

    Automated suites

    • Generation properties: at least 1,000 site seeds across terrain classes; no wet/cliff placement, protected-halo overlap, lot collision, invalid foundation, missing program room, fake door, inaccessible required anchor, or disconnected settlement entrance.
    • Determinism: same seed/version yields byte-identical site, plan, room, portal, household, workplace, and navigation signatures in Node, main thread, worker, reload, and differing query order.
    • Portal matrix: player/NPC, public/private/guest/workforce, open/closed/locked/blocked, two-way crossing, stale reservation, simultaneous arrival, stream unload/reload, and feature rollback.
    • Route properties: every home bed, workplace anchor, public service, and settlement exit is reachable by its authorized actors; every regional-to-room route has explicit handoffs and no positional discontinuity.
    • Household invariants: one canonical home per resident, one household membership unless explicitly displaced, capacity-respecting beds, valid relationship endpoints, witnessed memories only, and no duplicate rumor effects.
    • Routine soak: 64 detailed NPCs plus summarized town population for 1,000 world hours with weather, commitments, groups, doors, service shifts, household episodes, culling, and repeated reload; zero lost/duplicated occupants, deadlocks, double outcomes, or unbounded logs.
    • Browser vignettes: approach farmstead, enter/exit each program, follow a resident from bed to work, watch a shop open, arrive after a missed shift, receive an invitation, test trespass response, and cross the same door behind an NPC.
    • Rendering regression: exterior/interior culling, roof policy, shadows, portal visibility, world-floor agreement, collision, desktop/XR tiers, and existing wilderness/rail/cave benchmarks.

    Debug surfaces

    • World-site overlay: cell, score, rejection reason, founding reason, entrance, exclusion, trail connector, plan hash.
    • Settlement plan overlay: district/lot bounds, circulation, building programs, room graph, portal access, floor claims, collision, action anchors.
    • Resident inspector: household/home/bed, workplace/shift, current location reference, route legs, active routine/commitment/activity, authorization.
    • Portal inspector: discrete state, animation target, reservation, collider, authorized sets, last event receipt.
    • Streaming counters: summaries/plans/buildings/interiors/avatars active, queue depth, assembly ms, draw calls, triangles, collision claims, snapshot bytes.
    • Deterministic jumps: nearest farmstead/hamlet/village/town, occupied home, worker commute, active shop, private threshold, open-question stress fixture.

    Risks & open questions

    RiskProcedural validity collapses at scale. A 95% valid room/lot solver becomes unusable across a town. Use deterministic fallback plans per program/class, reject candidates before mesh build, and gate every phase on zero invalid required structures across the seed corpus.

    RiskAll-enterable buildings exceed rendering or content budgets. Stream interior cells only near active portals, reuse furnishings/modules, cap active interiors, and keep every canonical interior data-valid even when not rendered.

    RiskThresholds become collision and crowding traps. Use capacity-one reservations, approach bays, expiry, alternate waiting anchors, door-clearance validation, and a recovery path that moves an actor to the nearest valid side without teleporting across the portal.

    RiskTowns feel empty or overactive. Select detailed residents by visible purpose, group distant people into authored low-cost crowd cues only if needed, and favor staffed services/commutes over random wandering. Density is tuned per tier, not solved by spawning everyone.

    RiskFamily labels become insensitive or mechanically uniform. Support multiple adult household forms, separate role labels from affection, let relationships vary, avoid assuming every adult is partnered, and review authored dialogue/trespass consequences.

    RiskGenerator updates corrupt lived history. Version plans, hash semantic topology, persist sparse deltas with explicit references, test migrations, pin old versions when mapping is unsafe, and surface orphan audits.

    RiskSettlement sprawl erases wilderness rhythm. Use coarse placement, strong rejection zones, approach corridors, large gaps, and class rarity. A town is a destination, not background coverage.

    Recommended sequencing

    Phase 2 deliberately cuts through every risky seam with one small farmstead before generalizing any catalog. Do not begin large-settlement layout before portal, room, household, and routine contracts are stable: multiplying an invalid doorway or ambiguous home across a town makes the problem exponentially harder to diagnose. Phase 6 may parallelize exterior LOD work with town plan grammar only after both consume the frozen Phase 3 plan/portal signatures. Keep the existing landmark, trail, station-resident, and living-world readers available through the Phase 7 migration release.
