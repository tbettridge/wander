The lab's **Audit preserved crossings** button runs an isolated planning-worker audit for the selected fixture's region and lists each crossing's candidate or retention reason. It does not install a plan or authorize component migration. A candidate uses a provisional upstream head, so even a passing crossing is not yet an accepted drainage component. A traced legacy centreline is likewise only a survey: its fixed envelope is not proof that the entire old river footprint has been captured.

Crossing routes that converge on the same canonical routing node are audited as one drainage component. The graph is partitioned into reaches with each downstream edge assigned once. Bank and bed intervals are prepared at the fitted path's 4 m samples before the component's water levels are solved. Shared endpoints intersect their terrain intervals, and actual crossing intervals constrain the same solve. Coarse routing elevations are preferences, not frozen levels. Every resulting reach is checked against its intervals and grade, then every crossing is revalidated. Internal reaches keep full depth at junctions and do not force a sea-level endpoint. Any route, profile, bank or crossing failure retains the whole group. Confluence meshes and complete legacy-terrain replacement are still missing, so these descriptors cannot authorize activation.

The audit also performs a conservative connected-footprint search. Interval evaluation of the actual noise lattice includes the domain warp and excludes a cell only when its entire area is outside the legacy carve signal band. A separate source test can exclude rectangles whose projected legacy route is identically zero; it bounds both stages of the existing bilinear centre projection. Eight-neighbour traversal includes corner connections and wide side branches, grouping connected crossing anchors. A completed search establishes containment of this conservative support; it does not establish replacement feasibility or capture every crossing outside the supplied manifest. The search is limited to 8,192 cells, 250,000 bounds checks and 25,000 route-grid probes. Exhausted route probes fall back to the conservative signal test. Each component reports its own containment status, so a completed small component can be identified even when another component exhausts the overall search. Ocean carving is still part of the support, and a sampled dry location never serves as a boundary certificate.

## Implemented

- `hydrologyregions.mjs` installs multiple compatible rivers, closed basins and flowing lake systems per region. A denser 13 × 13 source survey probes nearby lower ground; ordinary bank, grade, cut and mesh gates remain unchanged. Failed lake outlets retain suitable closed basins. Each region has a 3,000,000-byte plan cap. The active nine-region field has a 32 MiB cap; non-regional fields retain their prior 16 MiB cap. The raw-candidate LRU holds at most 36 regions (108 MB serialized upper bound, excluding runtime object overhead and temporary planning data).
- Cross-border ownership is deterministic and independent of generation order. Whole components may extend up to 1,024 m outside their owning region. The eight neighbouring raw candidate sets therefore contain every possible overlap; stable owner priority rejects conflicts consistently without clipping a channel at a tile edge. Components exceeding this finite extent reject. This provides bounded complete source-to-sea systems across region boundaries, not a parent drainage graph for arbitrarily large catchments.
- `HydrologyStream` keeps one active window, one in-flight request and only the newest desired destination. Stale replies, invalid identities and incomplete windows cannot publish. A 120-second timeout produces a controlled failure. A loading state protects travel beyond completed coverage and visible terrain replacement. Main-thread physics, the geometry workers and grass trail worker all consume the same published field. Worker epochs prevent obsolete geometry from returning after a plan switch. Crossing and settlement caches include the current water identity.
- Regional waterside destinations improve actual discovery: at most two suitable pond/lake shore targets per planning region connect to the established landmark trail network. Endpoints are dry, near visible water and on gentle ground. Faint spurs retain the existing terrain route solver; excessive grades or an unsupported required crossing reject the spur. Generation 2 and the fixed development fixtures retain their existing trail topology.
- Visual polish in the production river material: layered, distance-filtered ripple normals; separate channel and basin response; turbidity-dependent olive shallows and deeper blue-green water; stronger grazing-angle reflections; a narrow translucent shoreline fade; and reduced foam around sheltered lake margins. These are lighting changes, with no downward bending or displacement of the solved water surface. Inland channels retain their material away from the camera; ocean handoff still requires explicit sea ownership.
- One nearby planned lake receives a 384 × 384 reflection of actual terrain and vegetation. Captures are capped at 30 Hz while moving and 12 Hz while stationary, omit water and camera-attached objects, and use a 450 m far limit. The reflected image is distorted by the same ripple normals and restricted to the matching lake head and bounds. XR, underwater and distant views use the procedural sky reflection instead. The helper plane never becomes visible geometry. This is a desktop preview feature, not an XR performance certification.
- Irregular wet-bank pigments, deeper bed tint and deterministic shoreline vegetation in generation 3. Reeds cluster in sheltered shallow water with curved tapered leaves; occasional partly buried rocks sit on dry margins. Placement uses a global lattice and actual shared terrain/water queries, stays stable across chunks and requested LODs, and excludes trail approaches and landmark halos. Basin turbidity and exposure survive combined-mesh serialization and reach the shader.
- `planLakeSystem` adds bounded terrain-led inlet searches to a validated outlet component. Sources must start on suitable higher ground and terminate in the connected lake, sharing its exact fixed head. Each inlet is fitted and the entire enlarged lake/channel mesh revalidated before publication. Rejected additions leave the accepted component unchanged. Source count, route visits, inlet count and final mesh size remain bounded; inlet search has an 8,192-visit total cap. Same-lake inlet/outlet overlaps are allowed only inside connected lake water at the lake's fixed head.
- Outlet routing now tries up to 12 deterministic shore alternatives, selected from the refined connected shoreline with natural-terrain escape probes. Each route retains the 2,048-state per-attempt limit, with at most 8,192 total visits and 65,536 mesh vertices. This expands supported terrain without accepting excessive cuts, unsupported banks or a disconnected water surface. `scripts/check-water-coverage.mjs` provides a repeatable multi-seed, adjacent-region survey.
- Lake-aware outlet bank fitting refines connected basin membership onto the river's 2 m lattice. Sections touching known lake water share its fixed head; submerged lake margins do not require artificial raised banks. The deeper lake bed is retained during composition. Dry land and disconnected hollows retain ordinary bank constraints. Lake-supported reaches cannot be installed as standalone rivers or baked without their basin ownership.
- Sparse meshes compose basins and channels into one field, carrying lake/pond material identity and rejecting conflicting water heads or duplicate basin ownership. Connected lake membership now governs ownership; unrelated low hollows inside basin bounds no longer cause false head conflicts or acquire lake material. Level constraints use the renderer's exact section projection at every shared 2 m lattice vertex, closing gaps between lateral probes. Lake shoreline triangles must stay level, including after serialized-plan loading. A combined component must have a wet path from the basin to every channel root through actual mesh edges.
- `planBasinDrainage` routes from a surveyed or alternative shore anchor to an actual ocean cell, fits the entire outlet with a fixed lake head and sea-mouth collar, and bakes the shared mesh only after all gates pass. Seed 4242's pond at (288, 3912) passes with 212 search states and 12,527 vertices. The planner can choose a smoother escape than the survey's jagged priority-flood path; all new excavation remains subject to full bank and cut limits. `plan-basin-drainage-preview` now plans optional inlets too, choosing the first validated basin in a region or a requested basin ID and rejecting when none pass.
- Full bank fitting of the default four outlet surveys finds two supported pond outlets and two routes whose bends are too tight for the complete bank width. Refining lake membership recovers a valid contact missed by the coarse grid. The lab's outlet report now distinguishes these results from centreline survey success. Extending the surveyed outlets toward the sea also exposes further fitting failures; no flowing lake is silently activated from a short survey.
- Basin outlet survey in `basinoutlet.mjs`: follows the priority-flood escape tree from the lake to a lower external valley, retaining only the final exit when the tree crosses dry terrain between lake arms. Survey edges, exhausted paths, uncontained basins and excessive sill excavation reject. A cut-only centreline profile starts at the exact lake head and descends within the ordinary grade limit. This does not validate full-width banks or claim a source-to-sea connection.

The default network uses 22,355 vertices instead of a bounding rectangle exceeding the 65,536-vertex limit. Empty space between arms owns no water. Only wet regions connected to channel centres are included; disconnected low hollows beyond dry bank crests are excluded without changing any incident visible water triangle's solved head. An open wet boundary still rejects.
- Explicit sea-mouth collars constrain the final 64 m to the solved zero head. Bed conditioning returns to natural submerged ground at the outlet, with estuary ownership carried through serialization and the production shader. Sparse boundaries require an unchanged dry collar, or unchanged submerged ground at a zero head with full ocean ownership. The new mesh remains cut-only and validates its vertex budget, arrays, bounds, ownership and collars before installation. General raised-bank composition remains unfinished.
- Extraction of realized crossing construction recipes, versioned JSON manifests, immutable validation and restored runtime route indexes. Four original complete scatter payloads remain byte-for-byte identical. Restored route/crossing lookup and conflicting duplicate rejection are tested.
- Terrain-derived connected closed basins with deterministic identity, fixed water levels, shoreline grids and natural bathymetry. Existing crossing approaches, trail strips, landmark/settlement sites and known railway corridors are excluded during planning.
- Basin terrain, water mesh, walking depth and underwater identity share the same field. A canonical 4 m terrain lattice preserves the basin grid's shoreline triangles across requested LODs.
- Water kind, flow, turbidity, exposure and sea ownership reach the geometry worker and shader. Inland basin water does not disappear through ocean-distance ownership. Bank pigments and clustered sheltered reeds are included.
- Worker plan hashes, stale-result rejection and bounded initialization retry. Lab fixture replacement publishes complete terrain and water together.
- Bounded terrain-led candidate routing to actual ocean cells, canonical boundary portals, downhill interval fitting, metre-based river sections, continuous bend-dependent bank profiles, and cut/fill constraints.
- Narrow river candidate sampling and rendering through the same serialized field and actual worker, with a canonical 2 m terrain grid. Water is level across each analytical section; mesh shoreline contacts are tested against the exact terrain triangles.
- A shared junction-level solver and deterministic route-graph merge, including incompatible tributaries and ambiguous downstream ownership rejection. These are not yet the activated network planner. Overlapping reach descriptors are rejected until junction geometry is implemented.
- Deterministic crossing migration reports with manifest integrity checks, exact crossing anchors, water-level/clearance constraints and all captured dry approach samples. Long bridge spans are not treated as channel widths. Reports remain explicitly ineligible for activation.
- Routing retains incoming-direction states, preserves fractional source identity, rejects spatial loops and handles sources already in the ocean. Fitting checks bend radius against the actual bank and blend envelope and rejects degenerate paths.
- Planning-only connected legacy footprint descriptors, deterministic component identities and worker transport. Synthetic closed, branching and corner-connected footprints are covered; infinite/over-budget searches cannot claim closure. Runtime generation remains unchanged.
- Shared crossing-route component audits. Routes are grouped by actual canonical-node convergence, junction heads are solved across the group, and every member is refitted and revalidated before the component can be called a candidate. Failure is reported for the whole group and activation remains disabled.
- Terrain-first component fitting in `rivercomponent.mjs`: prepared bank intervals, shared endpoint constraints, one graph-wide level solve and validated final reach descriptors. Solving coarse preferred heights first caused false rejections and has been removed. Routing also penalizes abrupt terrain drops as a heuristic; fitted terrain constraints remain authoritative.
- Dry captured approach elevations now constrain bank fitting before water levels are selected. The interpolated bank floor must remain within the 0.25 m support tolerance and above the water. Landings inside a proposed channel require rerouting; fixed bank heights incompatible with an approach are rejected. Component fits consider dry samples from all supplied crossing entries. The constraint on both adjacent heads is conservative and can reject feasible alternatives.
- Internal reaches explicitly disable estuary fading, preventing an inland confluence from handing its water surface to the ocean shader.
- Optional finite junction collars in component fitting: all fitted sections within the collar share one water head, enforced inside the existing terrain-interval solve. The default remains unchanged for historical migration comparisons. Edge-specific maximum drops can tighten, but cannot relax, the ordinary grade limit.
- Conservative confluence-region preparation in `riverjunctions.mjs`. It includes bank/blend envelopes, requires shared ownership and a common head throughout every potentially overlapping segment, rejects overlaps outside a valid confluence and refuses intersecting junction regions. Region preparation alone remains ineligible for activation; the bounded mesh prototype below consumes these checks.
- Bounded cut-only component meshes in `rivercomponentmesh.mjs`: a shared 2 m grid unions incident channels and rounded endpoint sections, with one head through the junction. Version 2 stores original natural elevations and clips bank profiles to natural ground, avoiding unnecessary rejection when a bank can meet existing ground without fill. Excess cuts and uncontained water still reject. Grid bounds, alignment, finite arrays, identity, and an unchanged dry outer collar are validated before installation; the collar blends back to runtime natural ground.
- Joined components install through the shared `WaterField` and existing serialized geometry-worker protocol, so terrain, water and walking queries use the same field. Installation is restricted to generation-3 preview plans. Duplicate reach ownership and unresolved component/reach/basin overlaps reject atomically. Component bounds select the canonical 2 m terrain lattice. The lab exposes the real-terrain junction fixture; automatic fresh-region network assembly, ocean handoff and raised-bank composition remain unfinished.
