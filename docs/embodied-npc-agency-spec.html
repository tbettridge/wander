    Technical design · living world

    Embodied NPC agency

    • In review
    • Author Codex with Tim Bettridge
    • Reviewers Gameplay, narrative, animation, performance
    • Updated 2026-08-02

    TL;DR

    Make NPC intent legible in the body and consequential in the world: carried objects derive from real ownership, NPCs initiate bounded interactions for validated reasons, small groups coordinate shared travel, and situated actions react to weather and place. Add one deterministic activity arbiter over the existing commitment, journey, relationship, and event ledger; keep the language model read-only and presentation non-authoritative. Ship in eight gated phases, beginning with state-budget headroom and a letter-prop vertical slice, then interactions, pairs, groups, environmental actions, and default-on integration.

    Context & problem

    Fable now knows why an NPC is travelling and what happened when they arrived, but much of that agency is still invisible until the player opens dialogue or inspects debug state.

    The current living-world branch already provides stable NPC identity, concrete commitments, exactly-once outcomes, directed relationships, sourced memories, semantic rumor exchange, off-screen journeys, grounded dialogue, and versioned persistence. The rendering layer also has static identity accessories—cases, baskets, lanterns, staffs, books—and hand-bone attachment points. These are strong foundations, but they do not yet compose:

    • Intent is not embodied. A courier can own a specific letter in authoritative state while displaying the same authored accessory they always wear. Transfers change data, not hands.
    • Encounters are reactive and generic. Travellers notice, glance, or briefly stop for the player, but cannot choose a reason, approach, offer a bounded interaction, or remember its outcome.
    • Travel is individual. Each journey owns its own route progress; two people cannot rendezvous, share pace, argue, escort one another, or split without drift and duplicated authority.
    • Place does not invite behavior. Weather, streams, trail crossings, junctions, platforms, and train schedules exist, yet NPCs mostly walk, loiter, converse, or repair a commitment asset.

    If these features are added as independent animation tricks, they will contradict the ledger and disappear off-screen. If they are added as free-form model agents, they will be unbounded and unreplayable. The problem is therefore not content quantity; it is a shared, deterministic representation of what an NPC is doing now, why, with whom, where, and what can interrupt it.

    ConstraintThe current 64-NPC/1,000-hour compact snapshot is 252,603 bytes against a 256,000-byte target. Phase 0 must create headroom before persisting new collections; adding schemas first would knowingly break the existing budget.

    Goals & non-goals

    Goals

    • Make every visible intent prop correspond to a real item, payload, tool requirement, or durable personal aid.
    • Allow an NPC to initiate one dismissible, reason-grounded interaction without stealing camera or input control.
    • Support deterministic pairs and temporary groups of two to four people through meet, travel, pause, conflict, split, and escort episodes.
    • Run seven situated action types only at valid spatial anchors and environmental conditions.
    • Preserve off-screen correctness, save/reload, exactly-once consequences, authored fallback, and instant feature rollback.
    • Stay within the existing persistence, simulation, grounding, draw-detail, and XR population budgets.

    Non-goals

    • A free-form LLM planner, model-authored world state, or autonomous tool calling.
    • A general physics inventory, crafting system, economy simulation, or arbitrary object pickup.
    • Crowds larger than four, permanent parties, tactical formations, combat, or crowd avoidance.
    • Forced dialogue, camera capture, blocking cutscenes, violence, or unavoidable confrontation.
    • Full NPC train boarding/riding in v1; waiting responds to the timetable, but boarding is a later rail integration.
    • Bespoke mocap, voice, cinematic hand IK, seating, or terrain-wide authored action markers.
    • Replacing static identity accessories; they remain the fallback when no higher-priority intent prop occupies the slot.

    Experience principles and scope

    Legibility before variety

    A player should first understand “courier with a letter,” “two people travelling together,” or “someone sheltering from a storm.” More catalogs do not help if silhouettes and timing are ambiguous. Each feature ships through a narrow vertical slice before adding variants.

    Sparse initiative, easy refusal

    Most NPCs still pass by. At most one NPC may offer the player an interaction at a time, offers expire, and dismissing or ignoring one has no hidden penalty unless the player explicitly made a promise. An NPC never auto-opens dialogue or interrupts another conversation.

    World facts own outcomes

    The event ledger owns item transfers, relationship deltas, group membership, action completion, and commitment effects. Animation may arrive late or be culled; the model may phrase a warning or greeting; neither can create the fact.

    Interruptions are first-class

    Rain ends, trains leave, players walk away, deadlines become urgent, targets move, actors unload, and groups disagree. Every episode defines interruption and recovery rather than assuming a perfect performance.

    Activity arbitration

    NpcActivityArbiter evaluates data-only candidates at low cadence and chooses at most one foreground activity per NPC. It does not invent goals; it accepts candidates from commitments, interaction policy, group coordination, and the situation index. Priority is fixed:

    1. Invalid-state recovery and safety (missing entity, bad anchor, group leader gone).
    2. Player dialogue already in progress and authoritative outcome handoff.
    3. Accepted player interaction or required group rendezvous/split.
    4. Active commitment journey and deadline urgency.
    5. Situated action or pending unsolicited interaction.
    6. Ambient group conversation, loiter, and platform wander.

    Foreground activities expose enter, tick, interrupt, resumePolicy, and complete. Candidates carry a stable ID, reason reference, earliest/latest world hour, location constraint, prop requirements, and interruption mask.

    Visible intent objects

    Generalize ownership, not inventory simulation

    Add a compact projections.items registry. Existing letters migrate into item instances while the letter projection remains as a compatibility reader for one release. Goods may remain stack projections for station stock, but a visible parcel or basket is a concrete container item that references its contents.

    ItemInstance {
  id, kind, ownerId, quantity, condition,
  purposeRef: { kind, id },        // commitment/action/identity
  contentsRef?, slotHint?, twoHanded?,
  createdEventId, lastTransferEventId
}

NpcLoadout {                       // derived, never authoritative
  actorId, leftHand?, rightHand?, backHip?, overlay?
}

    One item has exactly one owner. Transfer reducers update ownership once, then emit a bounded presentation cue. A culled handoff still succeeds; when either participant next renders, loadout derivation shows the new owner. A missing mesh never blocks an outcome.

    Animation composition

    Extend applyPose with prop constraints after base gait but before gestures. Two-handed props replace arm swing; one-handed props reserve a grip and redirect gestures/pointing to the free hand; walking sticks add a planted support phase; maps require a stopped two-hand pose. Near meshes use shared geometries/materials and existing detail thresholds. Far NPCs retain silhouette-only props, with no lights, tool parts, or grip solving.

    NPC-initiated interactions

    Interaction lifecycle

    planned → approaching → offered → engaged → resolved
    └───────────────→ expired / declined / interrupted

    An InteractionEpisode records initiator, player target, kind, stable reason reference, place, choices, expiry, cooldown, and outcome. Planning occurs at most once per second. A global player-attention budget permits one offered or engaged episode, enforces a 90-second minimum between unsolicited offers, and blocks initiation during dialogue, menus, train boarding, unsafe traversal, or another NPC’s approach.

    The NPC may approach only through existing platform wander or journey stop mechanics. At offer range they face and gesture, deliver one authored/model-optional bark, and display a prompt such as “T — hear the warning” or “T — respond to Maren.” Dialogue never opens automatically. Walking away, declining, or waiting 12 seconds expires the offer cleanly.

    Dialogue and safety rules

    • The opening prompt receives the exact interaction kind, reason, participants, evidence/provenance, permitted choices, and relevant commitment/item IDs.
    • Authored fallback covers all six kinds. If the model fails or is disabled, the episode remains fully usable.
    • Confrontation never blocks locomotion, threatens violence, penalizes non-response, or derives charges from model prose or multi-hop rumor.
    • Player text is remembered as a claim. Only explicit bounded choice controls invoke reducers.

    Travelling pairs and temporary groups

    Group data, individual responsibility

    A TravelGroup owns coordination—not personal outcomes. Each NPC keeps its own commitment and item ownership; group membership records why they are together and which shared route segment they coordinate.

    TravelGroup {
  id, state, leaderId,
  members: [{ npcId, role, joinedAtHour }],
  episodeKind, destinationKey,
  sharedProgress, formationSeed,
  startedAtHour, expiresAtHour,
  splitPolicy, latestEventId
}

    States are forming, together, paused, splitting, and dissolved. Membership is exclusive: one NPC may belong to at most one active group, a group has two to four live members, and every member references the same group ID.

    Formation and movement

    Compatible NPCs may form a pair when co-located, socially eligible, and sharing a destination or route prefix. A rendezvous action brings them within range without teleporting. Once together, one shared journey clock advances the route at the slowest member’s pace; member positions derive from bounded along/across offsets around that progress. Off-screen simulation advances one route, not N nearly identical routes. Visible grounding still resolves each body against the shared walkable surface, within the existing sample budget.

    If a member is missing, tombstoned, held by player dialogue, or unable to restore the route, the group pauses briefly and then applies its split policy. The leader is deterministically re-elected if needed. No follower chases forever.

    Risk score

    Risk is deterministic route data: grade, unbridged ford count/depth, storm, darkness, and distance to the next safe anchor. It is a reason for coordination, not a combat system. The score is sampled when planning a segment and only reconsidered at anchors or material weather transitions, preventing per-frame regrouping.

    Situated actions

    Action anchors

    World generators publish compact ActionAnchor records into a spatial index. Anchors are derived from existing deterministic structures and rebuilt from stable source IDs; geometry is not duplicated in the living-world snapshot.

    ActionAnchor {
  id, kind, sourceId, x, y?, z,
  capacity, facing?, tags,
  validFromHour?, validToHour?
}

SituatedAction {
  id, actorIds, kind, anchorId,
  state, reasonRef, propIds,
  startedAtHour, completesAtHour,
  interruption, effectEventIds
}

    Action states are planned, approaching, acting, completed, interrupted, and expired. Purely ambient completion may only emit a bounded timeline cue; any item condition, relationship, commitment, or player-help effect goes through applyLivingWorldEventOnce.

    Anchor producers

    • Rail stations: platform wait zones, building eaves, benches only if a seated pose is later supported.
    • Trail graph: junctions, route-class changes, existing crossing endpoints, landmark clearing edges.
    • River/trail crossing analysis: safe bank samples only; never infer a drinking site from render water alone.
    • Commitment projections: maintenance assets, repair sites, destination aftermath props.
    • Weather and rail schedule: shared conditions layered over anchors; no new per-NPC environmental sampling.

    Event vocabulary

    Use stable IDs derived from episode and transition IDs: item.transferred, interaction.offered, interaction.resolved, group.formed, group.member-joined, group.split, action.started, action.interrupted, and action.completed. Only events with durable projections or memories require effect receipts; offer/animation telemetry remains bounded debug data.

    Migration

    1. Bump living-world state to v3 and preserve the current storage key for compatibility.
    2. Convert letter projections into item instances while keeping the v2 letter reader through the rollout release.
    3. Map static identity accessories to purposeRef: identity only when no authoritative intent item occupies that slot; do not persist duplicates.
    4. Restore old journeys without groups/actions exactly as today. New fields default empty and flags default off until their phase gate passes.
    5. On incompatible entity generation, retain the old blob and start a visibly versioned state as existing policy requires.

    Backout

    Add independent flags: intentPropsEnabled, npcInitiationEnabled, travelGroupsEnabled, and situatedActionsEnabled. Disabling a consumer stops new episodes, resolves or safely splits active transient behavior at the next stable anchor, and preserves snapshot data. Dynamic props fall back to static accessories; journeys resume individually; existing commitments/outcomes/social memory remain enabled.

    Presentation and player experience

    One foreground activity, multiple subtle layers

    The actor may still carry an item, look at weather, and walk in formation, but only one layer owns full-body timing. Composition order is base locomotion → formation/anchor position → prop grip → activity pose → interaction gesture/nod → gaze. Conflicting layers declare occupied hands, movement lock, gaze target, and facing target rather than overwriting bones ad hoc.

    Visible evidence after off-screen events

    Every consequential episode retains at least one bounded aftermath cue: a parcel at the destination, repaired equipment condition, NPC dialogue, group farewell memory, or sourced rumor. Presentation receipts prevent a handover animation from replaying indefinitely, but missing the animation does not undo the event.

    Interaction affordance

    Initiating NPCs use gaze, facing, a single gesture/bark, and an unobtrusive named prompt. The player keeps movement and camera control. The same keyboard/XR action that opens existing dialogue accepts the offer; a second explicit choice invokes any reducer. Accessibility settings should allow reducing unsolicited barks and extending offer timeouts.

    Model fallback parity

    Authored lines cover every interaction kind, group episode, and situated action. Prompts include source labels—“I saw,” “Bram told me,” “the station board says”—and may never substitute a target, item, route, weather state, train ETA, relationship evidence, or result.

    Testing and observability

    Automated verification

    • Item invariants: one owner per item; one item per occupied slot; transfers converge under duplicate/reordered/reload events; prop loadout matches ownership within one rendered frame.
    • Interaction state machine: enumerate every transition; reason/entity/choice references validate; one global offer; no auto-open; expiry/decline/interruption are terminal and replay-safe.
    • Group properties: unique membership, size two to four, stable leader election, shared route signature, bounded formation error, deterministic split, no deadlock under missing members.
    • Action matrix: each action across valid/invalid anchors, weather threshold hysteresis, deadline urgency, dialogue interruption, culling, reload, and feature disable.
    • Scenario integration: courier letter handoff, weather warning, accepted escort pair, argument/split, rain shelter, stream drink, map junction, boot repair, marker check, and train wait.
    • Soak: 64 NPCs for 1,000 world hours with weather transitions, train cycles, target movement, group churn, interactions, actions, and save/reload checkpoints.
    • Rendering: grip transforms, two-hand suppression, free-hand pointing, walking-stick plant, lantern LOD/light, culling restore, gait/grounding regression, XR resident cap.
    • Fallback: all offers and aftermath can be understood with the language model disabled.

    Debug surfaces

    • Selected NPC: foreground activity, commitment, loadout/ownership, interaction reason, group/role, action/anchor, interruption priority.
    • Item inspector: owner history, purpose reference, slot, condition, last transfer receipt.
    • Interaction inspector: eligibility score, evidence/provenance, attention-budget rejection, cooldown, choice/outcome.
    • Group overlay: leader, members, shared route progress, formation targets, drift, risk segment, pending split.
    • Action-anchor overlay: kind, capacity, producer/source ID, eligibility/rejection reason.
    • Aggregate counters: active props/offers/groups/actions, duplicate effects, interruptions, formation p95, snapshot bytes, simulation ms, extra ground samples, visible prop meshes/lights.

    Risks & open questions

    RiskToo much behavior becomes noise. Six offer kinds, group beats, and seven actions can turn a contemplative walk into a theme park. Mitigate with one global offer, long cooldowns, low action frequency, distance-aware presentation, and playtest-adjusted density—not more randomization.

    RiskAnimation layers fight. Props, gestures, groups, and activities all want hands/facing/speed. Mitigate with declared constraints and one activity arbiter; reject candidates whose required hands or motion mode are unavailable.

    RiskGroup deadlock or visual drift. A held/missing member could freeze everyone or followers could diverge on slopes. Advance one shared route off-screen, bound visible offsets, time out waits, and property-test member loss at every state.

    RiskProcedural anchors are unsafe or implausible. A river shader is not a drinkable bank and a roof mesh is not automatically reachable shelter. Anchor producers must use generation analysis and walkable-surface validation, then publish stable capacity-limited sites.

    RiskConfrontation becomes unfair or model-invented. Restrict v1 to directly observed ledger events/unresolved obligations, remain nonviolent and dismissible, expose evidence in debug, and disallow rumor-only accusations.

    RiskPersistence budget is already full. Gate all schema work on reducing the current soak snapshot to ≤220 KB, persist only active/latest records, compact new tuples, and fail the rollout above 256 KB.
